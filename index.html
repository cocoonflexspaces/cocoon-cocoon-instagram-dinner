What the evening is doing

The second annual production of a creative concept celebrating tastemakers in creative industries. Guests arrive into a cocktail reception, move through product display and gifting vignettes, sit for a dinner that anchors the night, and circulate through photo moments — with a DJ or entertainment element to be confirmed. The venue isn't a backdrop here; it's the anchor of the concept and theme.

What the venue needs to deliver

Enough distinct rooms or zones to build several vignettes around a central dining experience for up to 70 seated. Texture and architectural character that photographs — more dynamic than a white-box institution. Load-in access the day before, catering-capable back of house, power and placement for lighting and A/V, and a Manhattan address — or a Williamsburg / Greenpoint one that's an easy ride from it.

Included with every venue in this deck — and answers to your production questions

On-site Casa Manager for every booked hour — vendor coordination, deliveries, on-the-day problem-solving — plus a complimentary site visit + tech scout before the run, and 2 hours of Cocoon Concierge sourcing (caterer, DJ, florals, photographer, security) baked in.

No required vendor partners — Cocoon works with an open vendor pool; your production team and preferred caterers are welcome (COI required). Floorplans with dimensions and ceiling heights are available for every venue on request — several appear below. A/V and rigging vary by property and are noted per venue; where there's no house system, Cocoon coordinates rentals. Two-day rentals with prior-day load-in are standard practice at every property in this deck, subject to calendar confirmation once the date locks.

Venue A · Landmark off Fifth · Upper East Side, Manhattan · Discovery Portfolio

A landmarked Beaux-Arts exhibition house, steps off Fifth Avenue.
Three gallery floors, a carved Stone Room, and a covered porte cochère for arrivals.

Floor 2 · Stone Room
The oval stair

Commissioned in 1913 and built as a place to show things — a library, exhibition hall and sculpture studio — fully restored and reopened in 2021 as a contemporary exhibition space, directly across from the Guggenheim. Three floors of museum-grade galleries, each with its own temperament: a white-box ground floor on original penny tile, the signature Stone Room under 17′10″ ceilings (carved Caen stone, original checkered marble, arched windows over the street), and a top floor that pairs a fully wood-panelled salon with a window-lined white gallery.

For this evening: cocktails in Gallery 1 off the porte cochère arrival, dinner for 70 in the Stone Room + Gallery 2 sequence (the second floor connects as a single ~2,000 sq ft run — cocktails spill one way, dinner sits the other), gifting and product vignettes in the Wood Room one floor up. The oval stair and the sculptural cobalt-tiled restroom are photo moments guests find on their own. If the team wants the closest spiritual successor to the Brandt Foundation — institutional gravitas, but warmer and far more textured — this is it.

Venue B · Casa Joy · Murray Hill, Manhattan · 5,600 sq ft · up to 75 guests

A 19th-century brownstone where whimsy meets grandeur.
Five stories of ornate detail, an Art Nouveau staircase, and a rose garden.

First floor · Salon fireplace
Second floor · Piano room

Built in 1850, renovated in 1900, once home to a member of Herman Melville's family and later a candy magnate — now a 5,600 sq ft, five-story townhouse infused with the artistic vision of its current owner. Ballet-slipper pink walls, original wood paneling, a curved Art Nouveau staircase with iron balustrade, a Murano-glass chandelier under vaulted 10.5–12.5 ft ornate ceilings, and a giant marble basin and fountain from India. Every corner is a vignette that's already been built.

The evening maps naturally: cocktails in the grand reception hall and lounge, dinner anchored in the dining room and reception hall sequence on the parlor floor, gifting vignettes in the library and the fourth-floor painting studio, and the rose garden + patio as an open-air photo and arrival moment. At up to 75 guests, the 70-seat dinner is an intimate, house-party-scaled takeover. Because the house runs vertical, the dinner wants a creative layout — salon-style tables threaded across the connecting parlor-floor rooms, or a split-course format that moves guests between floors (a progressive dinner is very on-concept for a tastemaker crowd). That layout study is exactly what we'd walk on a site visit.

One more lever worth knowing: the art collection can be thinned or fully removed for the night. We're adding photography of the rooms with the art taken down to this deck shortly — the house reads dramatically cleaner and more minimal without it, so the team can dial the eclecticism up or down to match the concept's scenography.
